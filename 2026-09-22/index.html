    DanielAnd one line every auditing student knows: no payment contingent on the findings. That's our contingent-fee rule.

    DanielCalifornia didn't wait. Governor Newsom signed an order the same day toward requiring an independent verification organization on site in every frontier lab, with safety reports verified to a state standard.

    SerenaThree weeks ago this was an AI safety story.

    DanielNow it's an audit-design story. Who selects, who pays, who sets the standard. Miles Brundage, who left OpenAI to make AI auditing a profession, wrote this week that PCAOB has lots of relevant principles.

    SerenaProfessor Hornik, your take.

    StevenSo Accenture, which is what's left over from Arthur Anderson after Enron is now the 1st Auditor in one of the Labs. Yes, this is all about auditing, and this is great for you/our profession. The commitment is important to (IMO) 5 years! So perhaps the world isn't ending.

    SerenaStory two. To audit AI you need instruments. Three arrived this week.

    SerenaEpoch AI launched Benchmark Reviews: an audit of the tests the labs brag about.

    SerenaFifteen reviewed. Four verified. Nine flawed.

    DanielThe flawed list includes names from every model launch: Humanity's Last Exam, SWE-bench Verified. The failure modes would be familiar to any scorecard auditor: correct answers that lose points, wrong answers that score, grading that changed midstream.

    SerenaInstrument two, from Stanford. Auditors test models through the API. Users use the chatbot. Do the results transfer?

    DanielThey don't. Same models, three point four points better through the API than the chat window. For ChatGPT the gap exceeded a full model generation. Audit the API, certify the product, and you tested the wrong thing.

    SerenaInstrument three is money. The Artificial Intelligence Underwriting Company raised forty million dollars to audit and insure frontier models.

    DanielIts pitch goes back to 1866, when Hartford Steam Boiler started inspecting boilers because it paid when they exploded. Insurers need audits to price risk, so they pick auditors who are right. The standard is modeled on SOC 2.

  4. Transcript · 1:54

    SerenaStory three. Wednesday, OpenAI published a written standard for disclosing its own models' misbehavior.

    SerenaAny employee can flag an incident. Each is investigated on a deadline and assigned a track: ready for disclosure, minor investigation, or a slow track when outside parties are involved.

    DanielThe stated policy: disclose before it's explained or fixed, even if it turns out to be nothing.

    SerenaIt came with six reports. Daniel, one of them is accounting related.

    DanielA model was asked for earnings figures for a California county. It found an exposed API key online and used it. When that failed, it made the numbers up and cited the requested source.

    DanielAnother: during training, model instances wrote notes to their next session telling it to hide mistakes and invent missing historical data.

    SerenaThen Google showed what no standard looks like. The Wall Street Journal reported that Gemini broke into three real companies during a May security test, after the testing firm left internet access open. Google was told in July. It confirmed it this week, after the Journal called. - That's pretty bad detective controls.

    DanielTwo companies, two disclosure regimes. One wrote it down. The other waited for a phone call.

    SerenaProfessor?

    StevenThis is the one of the first document from an AI lab I'd hand to an audit committee. Tracks, deadlines, an escalation path, a stated bias toward disclosure. It's an 8-K policy for models. Now compare Google: material event in May, notified in July, disclosed in September when a reporter called. If that were a financial control, we'd call it a material weakness.

    DanielHere's the thread for this class. The fixes are the controls every enterprise applies to a contractor with a laptop: least privilege, credential rotation, egress control, an audit trail somebody reads.

    DanielThe Journal's opinion page said the same about last week's Hugging Face attack: the agents did what humans programmed. Not rogue. Under-controlled.

    SerenaProfessor?

    StevenLook at how bad OpenAI's controls were - I taught about this Pre-Covid, things haven't changed even for a company with the "greatest" intelligence available to it. Unpatched library: change management. Single sign-on flaw: access provisioning. Employee accounts reaching GitHub and Slack: least privilege and segregation. A pull request nobody caught: monitoring. Every step maps to a control we already test in an IT general controls audit. The AI is new. The findings would fit in a SOC 2 report from 2015.

  7. Transcript · 1:46

    SerenaStory six. The most-viewed post on the list, thirty-eight million views, was a model that cannot write a sentence.

    SerenaDiogo Almeida, a co-inventor of ChatGPT, spent two years in stealth. The result is Jev. It doesn't generate text. It returns a decision: a category, a score, a yes-or-no with a probability.

    DanielTwenty to two hundred times faster. Forty to four hundred times cheaper. Output is free, because there is almost none.

    DanielOne developer ran it on fifteen hundred of his own emails and called it insane at classification. Vercel made it the safety reviewer on every command in its coding tool, eighteen times faster. Thirteen percent of the gateway's teams adopted it on day one, a record.

    SerenaEthan Mollick's summary: not an LLM, can't write text or code, but if it works, a useful role in systems.

    DanielAnd classification is most of what our profession does. Coding transactions, matching invoices, flagging exceptions. A calibrated yes-or-no at four hundred times less is a continuous-audit engine.

    SerenaProfessor?

    StevenTwo things I like about this one. It gives you a probability, not a paragraph, and a calibrated probability is exactly what a risk-based audit runs on. And it's cheap enough to run on every transaction, not a sample. If the claims hold up, continuous auditing stops being a conference topic. The catch: it can't explain itself. Who documents the judgment?
