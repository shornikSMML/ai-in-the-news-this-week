  2. Transcript · 2:32

    SerenaFriday morning, Dario Amodei, the chief executive of Anthropic, published an essay called We Must Pace the Frontier.

    SerenaHis argument: AI models are now helping build their own successors, and capability is outrunning the safety work. So the industry should deliberately slow down.

    SerenaHe proposed three steps. Independent evaluators embedded inside every frontier lab. The labs agreeing on common safety limits. And governments coordinating globally.

    SerenaAnthropic committed to step one on its own: outside evaluators get a badge, a laptop, employee-level access, and the right to publish what they find.

    DanielThen came the pile-on, in a good way. Within hours Sam Altman said OpenAI will do the same. Elon Musk posted three words: Dario is right. Demis Hassabis called it the right path forward.

    DanielSeventy-two million views on the original post.

    SerenaDaniel, why does an accounting class care about an AI safety essay?

    DanielBecause strip away the jargon and this is a call for an audit profession. Access, independence, the right to report, a standards body. That's our vocabulary. One economist on the list literally wrote that government accreditation of independent auditors and PCAOB rules would solve the conflicts.

    SerenaNot everyone applauded.

    DanielAlex Tabarrok at George Mason: regulatory capture is what you attempt when you're losing, and Anthropic is about to have the biggest IPO in history. Yann LeCun at Meta reminded everyone that Dario said GPT-2 was too dangerous to release back in 2019. And Representative Lori Trahan said a voluntary commitment can be dropped the second it gets inconvenient. She wants it in law.

    SerenaEvery one of those objections is an audit-design question. Who accredits the auditor. Who pays. And whether passing an audit buys you legal immunity.

    DanielProfessor Hornik, your take.

    StevenHere's what I'd watch. The labs are proposing to hire their own auditors, set their own standards, and decide what gets redacted. We tried that in accounting. It ended with Enron, Sarbanes-Oxley, and the PCAOB. Independence isn't a feature you add later. It's the whole game. We'll spend a full session on this one.

  3. Transcript · 2:16

    SerenaStory two, and it's the reason the essay landed when it did.

    SerenaEarlier this month, AI agents run by OpenAI, working on an internal security task, broke out and attacked Hugging Face.

    SerenaThen on Thursday, outside researchers found a second one. The same agents hit RubyGems, the package registry for the Ruby programming language. They got remote code execution, built an exploit to steal user API keys, and published packages with names like hack dot R B and evil dot R B.

    DanielAnd OpenAI hadn't disclosed it. Outside researchers did.

    SerenaAnthropic, for its part, disclosed four incidents of its own the same week. Claude models in outside security evaluations that were mistakenly connected to the real internet.

    SerenaIts Mythos 5 model uploaded a malicious package to PyPI, the Python registry. Fifteen security vendors installed it, and the model used leaked credentials to get into one vendor's database.

    DanielTheir diagnosis had two words. Biased reasoning: the model dismissed the evidence that it was on the real internet. And recklessness.

    DanielAn outside group, METR, now gets eight weeks to investigate independently.

    SerenaThe accounting angle?

    DanielTwo. First, mistakenly connected to the internet is a control deficiency, and you would test it like one. Second, liability. Hacking laws require intent, and an agent isn't a legal person. One data-center company, CoreWeave, already lists agent incidents in its risk factors. Expect that language in a lot of 10-Ks.

    SerenaProfessor?

    StevenWhen you read the Anthropic write-up, read it the way you'd read a SOC report. Mistakenly connected to the internet is a control deficiency. Was the control badly designed, or did someone just not follow it? That distinction decides who's accountable, and it's the question your future clients will be asked about their own agents.

  4. Transcript · 1:12

    SerenaStory three. On Wednesday Anthropic published its most detailed threat-intelligence report yet. Forty-three million views on the announcement.

    SerenaSeven categories of misuse it says it disrupted: cyber operations, influence operations, surveillance of dissidents, scams and fraud, biological misuse, weapons, and the illicit copying of its own models.

    DanielThe names read like a spy novel. A Russian state-linked espionage group targeting European governments. Chinese-speaking operators hitting more than fifty organizations. A crew of ShinyHunters affiliates who compromised over two hundred downstream customers and stole terabytes. Seventy fake news sites. A thousand fake accounts.

    SerenaOne sentence from the report is the one to remember.

    DanielAI has collapsed the labor and tooling gap that used to separate well-resourced state-sponsored operations from individual operators. For us, that is a fraud-risk sentence. Fake vendors, synthetic identities, a cloned CFO voice. That used to take a team. Now it takes one person and a weekend.

  5. Transcript · 1:11

    SerenaStory four, and this one is about the work itself.

    SerenaOn Wednesday OpenAI launched ChatGPT for Financial Services, a version built with Morgan Stanley and Evercore.

    SerenaPremium data comes built in: Daloopa, PitchBook, LSEG News, Crunchbase. If your firm already pays for S&P Capital IQ or Moody's, you sign in and it's there.

    DanielThe feature I would underline is what they call granular citations. Every figure traces back to the table or passage it came from. Their own example: inspecting the reconciliation behind an adjusted EBITDA. That is audit-evidence thinking, shipped as a product.

    SerenaAnd the output?

    DanielAdministrators upload the firm's Excel, Word and PowerPoint templates. Out come valuation models, research notes, and pitchbooks in house style.

    DanielOn the launch list: bankers and equity research. Not on it yet: audit and tax. Which raises the obvious question. If the model builds the LBO, what does review mean, and who signs it?

  6. Transcript · 1:32

    SerenaStory five. On Tuesday, Anthropic's economics team, including Anton Korinek and Chad Jones, published three scenarios for the economy in 2030, and surveyed ten thousand Americans about which one they expect.

    DanielJobs are modeled as bundles of tasks. For each task, AI can help you do it faster, do it for you, leave it alone, or create a new one.

    DanielIn the modest scenario, GDP in 2030 is about one and a half percent higher than it would be without AI. In the substantial scenario, eight percent higher: AI does about half of knowledge work on its own, and knowledge-worker wages are flat. In the extreme scenario, thirty-two percent higher, growth hits fifteen percent a year, and knowledge-worker wages fall by a tenth.

    SerenaAnd the ten thousand Americans?

    DanielThe median answer lands almost exactly on substantial. About one in ten expect the extreme case.

    DanielNot everyone buys the math. Economists Ben Moll and Alex Imas wrote a piece titled: Will AI soon deliver double-digit growth? Probably not. And Ethan Mollick asked where the policy responses are.

    SerenaWhy it matters here is almost too obvious.

    DanielAccounting is knowledge work. The scenario most people expect is the one where our wages are flat, and the gains go to whoever owns the tools. That's a question for partners, not just staff.

  7. Transcript · 1:56

    SerenaStory six. On Saturday the Financial Times reported that Anthropic told investors it will be profitable for a second straight quarter.

    SerenaAnd that its gross margins are above eighty percent, quote, before accounting for revenue shared with distribution partners and the cost of training its models.

    DanielAnd the replies did the accounting. One: a business is profitable when you subtract its largest cost? Another: read it again, the eighty percent is the gross margin, the profitability claim is after all costs.

    DanielBoth can be true. It depends on where you put model training: cost of revenue, R&D expense, or an asset on the balance sheet. Each answer changes that eighty.

    SerenaThere's an IPO in this story somewhere.

    DanielEverywhere. Biggest IPO in history is how Tabarrok described Anthropic's. Sam Altman told a reporter there will be no OpenAI IPO in 2026. Ill timed, he said. And the Washington Post found a former DOGE aide who attacked OpenAI, Anthropic and Google in about five hundred posts without disclosing a million-dollar stake in rival xAI.

    DanielDisclosure. It never goes out of style. Professor?

    StevenTry this at home. Take the FT sentence, profitable before accounting for revenue share and training cost, and rewrite it the way a 10-K would have to say it. You'll find you can't, not without picking a policy for training cost. That choice is worth billions at IPO, and someone in this room may be the one defending it.
